# CHAPTER 5: CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS

## 5.1 Introduction

This chapter presents the conclusions and recommendations derived from the findings discussed in Chapter 4. The conclusions are organised around the five research objectives, and the recommendations are designed to improve the effectiveness of school disciplinary procedures in promoting positive learner behaviour in Bulawayo Central District high schools.

---

## 5.2 Conclusions

### 5.2.1 Disciplinary procedures used in schools

The study concludes that schools in Bulawayo Central District use a variety of disciplinary procedures, including verbal warnings, written warnings, detention, removal from class, parental involvement, counselling referrals, and suspension for serious cases. These procedures reflect a structured but often reactive approach to student behaviour management.

The use of such procedures indicates that schools recognise the importance of maintaining order and instilling discipline. However, the procedures are not always applied consistently. This inconsistency weakens their effectiveness and reduces learner trust in the disciplinary system.

### 5.2.2 Learners' perceptions of disciplinary procedures

The study concludes that learners generally support the existence of discipline, but they expect it to be fair, respectful, and clearly communicated. Learners are more accepting of disciplinary procedures when teachers explain the reasons behind them and listen to their side of the story.

Learners are most critical of punitive or humiliating approaches that do not provide room for explanation. This suggests that discipline should be educational rather than purely punitive. Learners value procedures that teach responsibility and self-control.

### 5.2.3 Effectiveness of disciplinary procedures in promoting positive learner behaviour

The findings show that disciplinary procedures are only partially effective in enhancing positive learner behaviour. While they help maintain order in the short term, their long-term effectiveness is limited when they are inconsistent, harsh, or unsupported by counselling and parental involvement.

The study concludes that discipline is more effective when it is combined with explanation, counselling, restorative practice, and parental support. In this regard, discipline should aim to develop self-discipline and responsible behaviour, not merely enforce compliance.

### 5.2.4 Challenges associated with the implementation of disciplinary procedures

The study concludes that the major challenges facing schools include:
- inconsistency in application
- lack of trained counsellors
- inadequate teacher training in behaviour management
- large class sizes
- poor communication between school and parents
- limited monitoring and follow-up on disciplinary cases

These challenges limit the effectiveness of school discipline and reduce the likelihood of lasting behaviour change.

### 5.2.5 Strategies for improving disciplinary procedures

The study concludes that school disciplinary procedures can be improved through a holistic and learner-centred approach. This includes:
- clear and documented disciplinary procedures
- teacher training in positive behaviour management
- stronger Guidance and Counselling services
- parental involvement and regular communication
- use of restorative practices and mediation
- consistent monitoring and evaluation of disciplinary cases

The study further concludes that a school disciplinary system that combines consequences with support, explanation, and counselling is more likely to promote positive learner behaviour than a system focused solely on punishment.

---

## 5.3 Recommendations

### 5.3.1 Recommendations for School Administration

1. Schools should develop clear and documented disciplinary policies that are understood by all teachers and learners.
2. Discipline should be applied consistently across all classes and by all teachers.
3. School heads should monitor disciplinary cases regularly to ensure fairness and reduce bias.
4. A system of records should be kept for all disciplinary incidents to assist in follow-up and intervention.

### 5.3.2 Recommendations for Teachers

1. Teachers should use non-violent and respectful methods of discipline.
2. Teachers should explain the reasons for disciplinary action and give learners a chance to respond.
3. Teachers should use a progressive disciplinary structure, moving from warning to more serious interventions only when necessary.
4. Teachers should be trained in positive behaviour management and learner counselling skills.

### 5.3.3 Recommendations for Guidance and Counselling Departments

1. Guidance and Counselling services should be strengthened in all schools.
2. Counsellors should work with learners who display repeated behavioural problems.
3. Counselling should be used not only after misconduct but also as a preventive measure.
4. Guidance teachers should collaborate with class teachers and parents to support behavioural change.

### 5.3.4 Recommendations for Parents

1. Parents should be actively involved in school disciplinary interventions.
2. Regular meetings should be held between schools and parents to discuss learner behaviour.
3. Parents should support the school in reinforcing positive behaviour at home.
4. Parents should work with schools in addressing emotional or social issues affecting learners.

### 5.3.5 Recommendations for Policy and Education Authorities

1. The Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education should provide schools with standardised discipline guidelines.
2. Teacher training programmes should include behaviour management and counselling approaches.
3. Schools should be supported with human and material resources to strengthen Guidance and Counselling.
4. Education authorities should monitor the implementation of disciplinary policies to ensure consistency and learner protection.

---

## 5.4 Overall Recommendation

It is recommended that Bulawayo Central District high schools adopt a positive and restorative disciplinary framework. Such a framework should prioritise fairness, respect, learner participation, and the integration of Guidance and Counselling. While sanctions remain necessary for serious misconduct, they should be balanced with support, explanation, and rehabilitation.

In this way, school discipline will not only maintain order but also build responsible, self-disciplined, and academically engaged learners.
