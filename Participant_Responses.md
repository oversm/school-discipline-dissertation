# PARTICIPANT RESPONSES USED IN CHAPTER 4

## 1. Introduction

This section summarises the participant responses that were used to support the findings in Chapter 4. The responses were obtained from teachers, headmasters, parents, and learners in line with the methodology described in Chapter 3.

---

## 2. Teacher Responses

### Teacher T1
"When a learner misbehaves for the first time, we usually give a verbal warning. This allows them to understand that their behaviour is unacceptable without formal consequences."

"For learners with emotional or psychological issues, we refer them to counselling. This helps address the underlying causes of misbehaviour."

### Teacher T2
"Detention is used when verbal and written warnings have not worked. Learners stay after school or during breaks to reflect on their behaviour."

"Sometimes learners need more support than punishment, especially if their behaviour is linked to home or emotional problems."

### Teacher T3
"Written warnings are documented and filed. This helps us track patterns of misbehaviour."

"When a learner damages school property, we sometimes ask them to repair the damage or perform community service."

### Teacher T4
"The discipline system is somewhat effective, but only when consequences are consistently applied."

"Counselling would help, but we do not have enough support services in the school."

### Teacher T5
"When a learner is very disruptive, we remove them temporarily from class to prevent the problem affecting other learners."

"Large classes make it difficult to apply discipline consistently and fairly."

### Teacher T6
"Short-term, punishment works. Long-term, learners need guidance and emotional support to truly change."

---

## 3. Headmaster Responses

### Headmaster H1
"Verbal warnings are important because they give learners an opportunity to correct themselves immediately."

"Teachers refer cases to us when they cannot resolve them in class. We then investigate and determine appropriate consequences."

### Headmaster H2
"We use behaviour contracts for learners with persistent discipline problems."

"Consistency is essential; when disciplinary procedures are applied consistently, repeat offences tend to decline."

### Headmaster H3
"Suspension is used as a last resort for serious misconduct."

"Discipline is necessary, but punishment without education may not produce lasting behaviour change."

---

## 4. Parent Responses

### Parent P1
"I am aware that the school uses warnings and parental meetings when learners misbehave."

"The school should communicate with parents more regularly so that we can help our children behave well."

### Parent P2
"Some parents are not fully involved in school discipline. The school should find ways to work with families more closely."

### Parent P3
"Learners need guidance and counselling, not only punishment."

### Parent P4
"Discipline should be fair and should help learners understand what they did wrong."

### Parent P5
"Harsh punishment can make learners disrespectful or rebellious."

### Parent P6
"The school should listen to learners and explain consequences clearly."

### Parent P7
"If parents and schools work together, learners are more likely to behave well."

### Parent P8
"We need more communication between home and school."

### Parent P9
"Counselling can help learners deal with the causes of poor behaviour."

---

## 5. Learner Focus Group Responses

### Focus Group FG1
"When teachers explain why behaviour is wrong, it makes sense. But when they just shout, we feel disrespected."

"Some teachers punish certain learners and not others. That is not fair."

### Focus Group FG2
"We behave better when teachers are fair and listen to our side of the story."

"Punishment alone does not help; we need someone who listens and helps us understand why we misbehave."

### Focus Group FG3
"Discipline should educate us, not embarrass us."

"If teachers treat us with respect, we want to behave better."

---

## 6. Summary of Participant Responses

The participant responses show that:

- schools use a range of disciplinary procedures
- learners value fairness and respectful communication
- consistency is a major challenge
- counselling and guidance are important for behaviour change
- parents and schools need stronger cooperation
- discipline works best when it is educational and not purely punitive

These responses were used to build the findings and discussions presented in Chapter 4.
