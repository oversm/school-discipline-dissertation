# RESEARCH INSTRUMENTS

## 1. Introduction

This section presents the research instruments used in the study to collect data on school disciplinary procedures and learner behaviour in selected high schools in Bulawayo Central District. The study used two main instruments:

1. Semi-structured interviews
2. Focus group discussions

These instruments were selected because they allowed participants to provide detailed explanations and personal experiences related to school discipline.

---

## 2. Semi-Structured Interviews

### 2.1 Purpose of the Instrument

Semi-structured interviews were used to gather detailed information from teachers, headmasters, and parents about:

- the disciplinary procedures used in their schools
- the nature of learner misconduct
- their perceptions of the effectiveness of the procedures
- the challenges they face in implementation
- their suggestions for improving school discipline

### 2.2 Interview Schedule for Teachers and Headmasters

The following questions guided the interviews for teachers and headmasters.

#### Section A: Background Information
1. What is your position in the school?
2. How long have you worked in this school?
3. What is your experience with learner discipline?

#### Section B: Disciplinary Procedures Used
1. What disciplinary procedures are used in your school to manage learner behaviour?
2. Which procedures are used most often for minor misconduct?
3. Which procedures are used for serious misconduct?
4. Are disciplinary procedures documented in the school?
5. Do teachers follow the same procedures consistently?

#### Section C: Learner Perceptions and Behaviour
1. How do learners usually react to disciplinary procedures?
2. Do learners understand the school rules and consequences?
3. How do learners respond to warnings, detention, suspension, or counselling?
4. Are there learner behaviours that are more difficult to control?

#### Section D: Effectiveness of Discipline
1. In your view, how effective are the school disciplinary procedures?
2. Which procedures seem to lead to positive behaviour changes?
3. What evidence do you have that learners change behaviour after discipline?
4. Are there cases where discipline does not work?

#### Section E: Challenges in Implementation
1. What challenges do you face in implementing disciplinary procedures?
2. Are there issues with teacher consistency, parental cooperation, or counselling support?
3. What factors make discipline difficult to enforce?

#### Section F: Recommendations
1. What strategies would improve discipline in your school?
2. How can Guidance and Counselling support positive behaviour?
3. What role should parents play in improving learner discipline?

### 2.3 Interview Schedule for Parents

The following questions were used in interviews with parents:

1. What do you understand by discipline in school?
2. Are you aware of the disciplinary procedures used in your child's school?
3. How often are you informed about your child's behaviour at school?
4. Do you think these procedures are fair and effective?
5. What challenges do you face in supporting the school in disciplining your child?
6. What can the school do to improve discipline and positive learner behaviour?
7. Do you believe that Guidance and Counselling can help learners change behaviour?
8. What should the school do to build stronger parent-school collaboration?

---

## 3. Focus Group Discussions

### 3.1 Purpose of the Instrument

Focus group discussions were used to explore learners' experiences, perceptions, and feelings regarding school disciplinary procedures. This method allowed the researcher to capture shared opinions and group discussions around discipline and behaviour.

### 3.2 Focus Group Discussion Guide for Learners

The following guide was used:

1. What disciplinary procedures are used in your school?
2. Which of these procedures are fair or unfair?
3. Do rules and consequences apply equally to all learners?
4. How do teachers usually communicate discipline to learners?
5. Have you ever been punished for misbehaviour? How did it feel?
6. Do you think the discipline used at school helps learners behave better?
7. What makes discipline effective or ineffective?
8. Do you think learners need counselling or guidance when they misbehave?
9. What should schools do to improve discipline and promote positive behaviour?

---

## 4. Summary of the Research Instruments

The instruments used in the study enabled the researcher to collect both qualitative and descriptive data from multiple stakeholder groups. The combination of interviews and focus groups allowed for triangulation of information and deeper understanding of how school disciplinary procedures operate in practice.

The instruments were aligned to the study's five research objectives and were used to generate the findings discussed in Chapter 4.
